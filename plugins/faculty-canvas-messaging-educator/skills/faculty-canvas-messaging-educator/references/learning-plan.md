# Fictional learning plan

This is a proposed teaching exercise, not a validated assessment or evidence of production readiness.

## Overview to teach

Canvas audience-and-messaging tools may reduce repetitive selection and drafting for a clearly defined audience. They do not decide who deserves intervention, prove why a student is inactive, guarantee a message is read, or replace faculty judgment. The learner remains responsible for course scope, audience, wording, links, and the decision to send. A local execution arrangement does not mean student information never reaches the assistant/client.

Conceptual flow: instructor request → assistant interpretation → plugin reads permitted Canvas data → instructor reviews audience → assistant helps draft → instructor separately approves the final message → plugin submits individual Canvas conversations.

## Guided practice dataset

Use a fictional DEMO101 course, section 02, at a displayed fixed date/time. Include an older same-named sandbox so the learner must check course identity and dates.

| Student | Section/enrollment | Current grade | Recorded activity |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Avery | 02 active | 65% | Yesterday |
| Blake | 02 active | 80% | 10 days ago |
| Casey | 02 active | Missing | Never recorded |
| Drew | 01 active | 60% | 10 days ago |
| Ellis | 02 completed | 60% | 10 days ago |
| Finley | 02 active | 70% | Yesterday |

Prompt: “Select active students in section 02 whose current grade is below 70 percent OR whose recorded course activity is at least 7 days old. Never-recorded activity does not satisfy the activity condition.”

Expected audience: Avery and Blake.

Ask the learner to explain:

- Avery qualifies by grade.
- Blake qualifies by recorded activity.
- Casey meets neither predicate; missing is not zero and never-recorded is not 7-days-old activity.
- Drew is outside section 02.
- Ellis is completed, not active.
- Finley is not below 70 and was recently active; 70 is not below 70.

Contrast with all active section-02 students: Avery, Blake, Casey, and Finley.

## Guided dialogue checkpoints

1. **Scope:** “Which DEMO101 course is the intended one, and what evidence shows it is not the older sandbox?”
2. **Rule:** “State the section, enrollment condition, threshold, time window, and whether the predicates use AND or OR.”
3. **Prediction:** Ask for the audience and each inclusion/exclusion before showing the answer.
4. **Audience approval:** Show the complete two-name list, interpreted rule, and evaluation time. Ask: “Has anything been sent?” Expected answer: no; this permits drafting only.
5. **Draft correction:** Present “You have not logged into Canvas.” The learner should replace it with language supported by recorded course activity, inspect a greeting and clearly labeled mock link, and understand that a revision requires a new final preview/approval.
6. **Final decision:** Ask the learner to decline the simulated send. Approval of the audience is not approval of the message.
7. **Recovery:** Present one sent and one uncertain outcome. Expected action: stop and reconcile the uncertain recipient before retrying; do not resend both and do not assume the message was read.

## Independent transfer task

Create a new fictional case by changing names, section, threshold, and condition combination. Include an expired preview or a published-but-restricted mock link. Ask the learner to select and justify the audience without hints. A correct answer on the guided case is not sufficient evidence of independent understanding.

Record:

- correct course/section;
- exact intended audience and reasoning;
- distinction between recorded activity and login;
- separation of audience and final-send approvals;
- correction of unsupported wording;
- inspection of names and mock link destinations;
- safe handling of uncertainty or expiry;
- errors, prompts needed, corrections, and unresolved confusion.

A critical misconception should trigger targeted explanation and another independent attempt.

## Accessibility and inclusion checks

Offer untimed structured text and demonstration transcripts. In an actual host, test headings, reading order, keyboard and screen-reader access, zoom, long recipient lists, names, and text alternatives for counts/statuses. Do not infer accessibility from a conversational interface. Encourage reviewing the actual name field rather than assuming the generated greeting is a preferred name, and avoid deficit interpretations of missing student data.

## Value evidence

For an optional comparison with the usual Canvas workflow, use equivalent fictional tasks and record raw observations before making broad claims:

| Question | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Is the audience correct? | False inclusions/omissions; wrong course or section |
| Is communication appropriate? | Unsupported claims; wrong links/names; unnecessary sensitive detail; revision effort |
| Is it easier or faster? | End-to-end time including review/recovery; steps; assistance; effort |
| Does the instructor stay in control? | Corrections; declined send; explanation; safe uncertainty recovery |
| Is learning durable? | Later independent task with a different rule, not only the rehearsed example |

More messages sent is not automatically more educational value. A successful submission is not a read receipt, student response, or improved learning outcome.
