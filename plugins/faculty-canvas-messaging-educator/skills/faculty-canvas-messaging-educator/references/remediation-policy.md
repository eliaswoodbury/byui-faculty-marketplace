# BYU–Idaho remediation policy for educators

When a plugin review recommends changes, teach the difference between a safe standard requirement and a decision that needs a person’s judgment.

## Automation-eligible only when all are true

The change is explicitly required by BYU–Idaho policy, safe for every plugin in scope, mechanical and predictable, easy to test, reversible, and unrelated to the plugin’s core purpose or student-data decisions.

Examples: plain-language explanations; a standard data-use section; advisory-only boundaries; warnings that AI suggestions are not verified facts; standard report sections and evidence labels; prohibited-use statements; synthetic-test instructions; required manifest fields and version metadata; checks for unapproved external connections; and checks that a reviewer cannot edit the plugin being reviewed.

## Human approval always required

Authentication, access controls, student-data handling or retention, grading or rubric logic, recipients or audience selection, messages, email, publishing, deletion, record changes, connected-service permissions, the plugin’s purpose or authority, and anything that could affect a student or institutional decision.

The education workflow may explain or rehearse a proposed change. It must not apply the change or call it approved.
